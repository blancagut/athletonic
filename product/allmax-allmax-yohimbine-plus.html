ALLMAX Yohimbine+ delivers 3.0 mg of pharmaceutical-grade Yohimbine and 0.5 mg of Rauwolscine (Alpha-Yohimbine) per capsule, formulated for athletes and physique competitors during cutting phases and calorie-deficit cycles. Quick-Scan Benefits: Targets alpha-2 receptors to help support the release of stored fat during exercise Dual-compound formulation : Yohimbinel + Rauwolscine in every capsule Pharmaceutical-grade, cGMP-certified manufacturing : every lot tested for purity and potency Vegan & Gluten-free : 60 capsules per container What Is Yohimbine and How Does It Work? Yohimbine is a high-purity compound that works by blocking receptors called alpha-2 receptors found mostly in fat cells. These receptors act like an "off switch" that tells your body to hold onto stored fat. By blocking this switch, Yohimbine helps your body release stored fat more easily during exercise or when fasting. What Is Rauwolscine (Alpha-Yohimbine)? Rauwolscine, also known as Alpha-Yohimbine, is a rare close cousin of Yohimbine that stays active in your body longer and is stronger at blocking those same fat-storing receptors. Because it's more potent, you need less of it, which is why ALLMAX Yohimbine+ uses just 0.5 mg of Rauwolscine alongside 3.0 mg of Yohimbine in each capsule. The two compounds work together to support each other's effects. Yohimbine vs. Caffeine: A Caffeine-Free Alternative For athletes who want to cut back on caffeine but still need support for fat loss, Yohimbine works in a completely different way from caffeine. Caffeine works by blocking adenosine, a chemical in your brain that makes you feel tired. Yohimbine skips that entirely and instead targets the fat-storing receptors in your fat cells directly. Since ALLMAX Yohimbine+ contains no caffeine, it's easy to add to any supplement routine. Research on Yohimbine A randomized, placebo-controlled trial published in Research in Sports Medicine involving 20 professional soccer players found that body fat decreased from 9.3% to 7.1% after 21 days of yohimbine supplementation, whereas the placebo group showed no change. A separate clinical trial published in the Journal of Clinical Investigation reported that oral yohimbine administration in fasted healthy men was associated with a 40–50% rise in norepinephrine levels. Research suggests these findings support yohimbine's role in fat mobilization during exercise; individual results will vary.
